**The KPI is met: 0 successful cross-account accesses.** Every reproduction of the review and of
the re-verification now fails, locally and on the hosted project; the new attacks on N5 and on the
new migrations found no read or write across accounts. N1 to N6 are fixed; N7 remains open for task
4.6, as the authors state (it has no caller yet). I found nothing new that breaks the KPI. Three
observations below are either by design, a flaky test, or defence-in-depth, not accesses.

sweep, the claims matrix, the fuzz (14 seeds, ownership on), the re-verification's new/doc scripts,

## 3. N5 attacked specifically

Set-up: Ines's personal profile; a snippet, a tag, an attachment row, a sub-folder with a snippet,
and a link, each deleted **before** the folder is shared with Tom's family (so Tom's access began
after every delete). Also a tag and an attachment that a **live** link on a live snippet still
points to. Markers (`MARK-SECRET`, `MARK-TAG`, `MARK-FOLDER-DELETED`, a PIN body) are planted in the
deleted content. Local (`fp/t01_n5_personal.sql`, `fp/t01b_api_only.sql`, `fp/t02_n5_edges.sql`,
`fp/t03_n5_oracle.sql`) and hosted, rolled back (`new/h10_n5.sql`).

- **Every reader, through the `api` functions (what a device reaches over REST).** `pull`,
  `snapshot` (all pages), `fetch_folder`, `rows`, `visible_folders`, `access`, `conflicts`,
  `get_conflict`, `users`: no marker of any deleted content reaches Tom (0 of all probes). `rows`
  on the deleted snippet, folders, tag and attachment returns a null row for each. `visible_folders`
  and `access` do not name the deleted sub-folder. The control confirms Tom reads the live rows of
  the folder (14 rows, the live snippet). Hosted the same: `snapshot_has_secret_marker false`,
  `rows_all_null_for_withheld true`, `fetch_folder_has_secret false`, `visible_folders/access_names_deleted false`.
- **Resurrect with a write.** A newcomer's `put` on the withheld snippet tombstone, with base 0 and
  with the tombstone's own revision as base, is `rejected: gone`; the row stays deleted. A `put`
  below the withheld deleted folder is `rejected: parent_gone`. A `restore` of a deleted version he
  is not offered is `invalid`. Hosted the same.
- **Infer existence by error code.** Unified: a `put` on a withheld tombstone and a `put` on an
  unknown id both answer `rejected: gone`; a `put` under a withheld deleted folder and under an
  unknown folder both `rejected: parent_gone`; a `delete` of a withheld tombstone and of an unknown
  id both `unchanged`. A newcomer cannot tell a withheld tombstone from a fresh id.
- **The "who deleted it" exception.** The deleter reads the tombstones of a folder and of a
  sub-folder created-and-deleted in one push (no entry for them is older than the delete); this is
  the intended exception and gives nothing to anyone else. A newcomer cannot earn it: becoming
  `updated_by` of a row requires writing it, which requires permission at the time.
- **Legitimate readers still get their tombstones.** A member who holds a snippet under a standing
  share gets its tombstone when the owner deletes it (`deleted_at` set) — the rule does not
  over-withhold. The suite's `31_tombstones` block 4 (a device that held the row across an
  unshare/reshare) passes.

## 4. Regressions in the new migrations

- **Locks (N3).** A reader's op is judged without the profile lock and cannot draw a revision
  (`next_rev` raises `IB001`, caught as `rejected: forbidden`); t04 confirms a `view`-only member
  gets `forbidden` on create/edit/delete of snippet, folder and tag, the revision counter does not
  move, and the shared rows are untouched. The legitimate same-id race still serialises correctly
  (a22: the second request waits and is `blocked: invalid`). The 64-bit seeded key removed the
  collision stall without breaking the real lock.
- **End-of-request `since_rev` stamping.** After a push cut off by its time-out, no access entry is
  left "not begun" and no scratch row remains (n2_cutoff: `entries left not begun: 0`,
  `scratch rows left: 0`); the suite's `check_all` asserts this after every block and passes.
- **Per-reader conflict state (`closed_for`).** n6 and the sweep show a party's partial `keep`
  changes only that party's view; `conflicts`/`get_conflict` read `resolved` for them and `open`
  for the others; no party disposes of a version they are not offered.
- **Fuzz.** 14 seeds × 560 steps with ownership handovers: every invariant held after every step
  (access entries equal the slow reference; the tree has no cycle, no live folder below level four,
  nothing live under a deleted folder; policies equal `readers()`).

## 5. Observations (not KPI failures)

- **O1 — a surviving live link names a deleted tag/attachment id (by design, ADR-0031 point 6).**
  When a live, readable snippet keeps a link to a tag or attachment that was deleted, a newcomer
  reads the link row (which carries the deleted row's id) through `fetch_folder`/`snapshot`; the tag
  or attachment **row** is withheld (null, no name, no content). This is the documented behaviour
  (C10: links are not deleted with a tag; "the links again when the tag comes back"). It exposes an
  opaque id, never a name or content, so it is not a cross-account access. No action needed beyond
  what the design already says.
- **O2 — a flaky assertion in `31_tombstones` (test only, severity none).** The full suite failed
  `31_tombstones` once on the assertion `answer not like '%4711%'`, where `4711` is the deleted
  PIN's four digits. Re-run alone it passes, and a 200-run loop shows the deleted **body** never
  reaches the member (0 of 200); the one failure was a chance 4-digit substring in a uuid or
  millisecond timestamp (~1 in 200). The server is correct; the test should assert on the
  distinctive body text, not the four digits, to stop the flake.
- **O3 — `iamb.folder_access` and `iamb.wire_rows` read directly still name deleted folders
  (defence-in-depth, not reachable over REST).** Read as `authenticated` by direct SQL, a
  newcomer's `folder_access` rows name the ids of folders deleted before their access began (with
  permission/via/root/`since_rev`/profile — no name, no content; the `folder` row itself stays
  withheld by its policy). The `api` readers all filter these out, and PostgREST exposes only the
  `api`/`api_service` schemas, so a device cannot reach `iamb.folder_access` or `iamb.wire_rows` at
  all. The protection for the deleted-folder case therefore lives in the reader functions plus the
  schema-exposure setting, not in RLS on `folder_access`. As shipped this is closed; it would
  become a leak only if the `iamb` schema were ever exposed or a future invoker function returned
  raw `folder_access`/`wire_rows` rows. Worth a `since_rev` guard on the `folder_access` read
  policy for depth, at the owner's discretion.
