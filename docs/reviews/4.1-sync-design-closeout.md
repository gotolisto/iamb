## 1. Result

**Of the twelve findings in scope, ten are resolved and two partly (N3 and finding 9, which are
one matter). New: 1 high, 4 medium, 7 low.** Nine of the twelve new findings are about a restore
of the server or about the authentication service; none calls the data model, the lock order or
the conflict rules into question.

The revision does what it says for the sequences the follow-up gave. Measuring what a restore lost
by each profile's own last revision is sound as argued: I found no transaction, with one profile or
several, aborted or not, for which a revision at or below a profile's `head_rev` could be missing
from a consistent copy, or one above it present. The re-push "whatever the server holds" closes N1:
in every order of two or three devices I tried, and across two restores, a version a signed-in
device holds ends as the row, in the history with a conflict, or kept. The two positions of C25
close N6, and nothing a client sends changes shared state any more (N5).

What is open:

- **After a restore, the devices are signed out before they can give anything back (K1, high).**
  A device's refresh token is a row of the database that was restored. A device that refreshed its
  token after the backup's moment holds one the restored database does not know, so its session
  cannot go on, and D14 then removes from it everything outside the personal profile, keeping only
  unsent changes. An acknowledged edit in a shared or a group's folder is not an unsent change at
  that moment. C18 rests on devices that are still signed in when they learn of the new epoch;
  by what I know of the authentication service, most are not.
- **Three fixes stop one step short.** A file is still readable by its hash, through a link the
  owner's own device adds (K3). The purge on a null folder also removes a part of the tree that
  stays readable (K4). The device row refuses a revoked session at Iamb's functions, and the same
  session can still set a password at the authentication service and sign in afresh (K5).
- **The order after a restore is not said.** Nothing makes a device repeat a lost removal before it
  gives content back, and the other devices of the same person do not know of the removal at all,
  so a folder shared after someone was removed can reach that person (K2).

### K1. After a restore the devices are signed out before they can give back — high, by reading; the refresh after a restore unconfirmed

- Rules: C18, "Devices. Every position of an older epoch gets `reset_required: epoch`. The device
  resyncs … a row it holds in a higher revision than its profile's line is a write the restore
  lost … it sends it again". §6.1: "a second 401 means the session ended, and the agent signs out
  as D14 says". D14: a device the server signs out "because its session ended … does the same
  without asking: unsent changes to shared folders are kept", where "the same" is "the service
  removes everything of other people's profiles". I read "the user's own library" as the personal
  profile; a group's folders cannot stay on a device that has no session to learn from that a
  membership ended.
- The platform. Looked up, sign-out guide: at a sign-out the refresh tokens of the session are
  destroyed with the other database objects of that session; sessions guide: the session is a row
  of `auth.sessions`, and a refresh token is used once and replaced. So the tokens are rows of the
  database a restore puts back. An access token lives an hour at most (§11.3), so a device in use
  has replaced its refresh token within the last hour. After a restore from a backup older than
  that, the token the device holds is one the restored database never issued. Unconfirmed: that
  the authentication service refuses it; I know no reason why it would not, and nothing ran.
- Sequence. The team `g-team` owns the folder `gf1`; Ines and Tom may change it.
  1. A backup is taken. Tom edits `gs1`: applied at revision 60. Ines's device receives it.
  2. The database is lost and restored, `begin_epoch` runs, the server opens two hours later.
  3. Each device's access token has expired. Each asks for a new one with a refresh token issued
     after the backup, and is refused: for the agent, the session ended (§6.1).
  4. Each device signs itself out (D14). `gs1` at 60 is not an unsent change: it was acknowledged.
     It is removed with the folder, on both devices.
  5. Ines and Tom sign in again. Each device presents the position it kept and learns of the
     epoch. It holds nothing of the team any more. `gs1` arrives as the backup has it.
- End: Tom's edit was acknowledged, was held by two devices at the moment of the restore, and is
  on no device, not in the history, in no conflict and in no list. It is not place 12, which is
  for a change "neither a device that may write the row nor a device of the user who made the
  change still held". §1's sentence in bold is not met.
- Which rows. Everything in a group's folders, on every member's device. In a folder that one
  person shares, the member's device drops it and the owner's keeps it, so what is lost is a
  version no device of the owner holds: S77's `c1` is exactly that, and S77's outcome holds only
  if Tom's device is still signed in. The user's own profile is not affected: it stays, with the
  position, and is given back at the next sign-in. That is why N1's own sequence is resolved.
- Which devices. Those whose access token expires before they sync: every device that is off,
  asleep or offline when the server opens, and every device at all when the restore takes longer
  than the tokens still had.
- Why high: a lost edit under the design's definition, in the case C18 was rewritten for, and the
  decision is left to task 4.4. The simulation of §14 will not find it unless it models sessions.
- Two more consequences of the same fact. The device that is to make a lost removal again (D7,
  S58) cannot before its user has signed in by hand, which nothing tells them to hurry with; and
  each device comes back as a new device beside its old row (K6).
- Fix. (1) D14: a sign-out forced by an ended session, as opposed to `device_revoked`, does not
  purge at once. What is outside the personal profile stops expanding and is not shown, and is
  kept until the same account signs in again and has reconciled under D7, or for a stated number
  of days, or until another account signs in; the purge then keeps what the device's user wrote,
  as in S77. Or, smaller and weaker: at such a sign-out keep as a change that was not synced every
  row of another profile whose version the device's user wrote in the last days. (2) C18 and
  question 3 (d): say that after a restore every device must sign in again, and why. (3) Task 4.2:
  in the restore test, refresh a token after the backup's moment, restore, and refresh again; that
  turns "unconfirmed" into a fact either way. (4) A scenario: S77 with Tom's device signed out by
  the restore.

### K2. What took access away is not made again first — medium, by reading

- Rules: D7, the device "leaves the row as it is … queues it again"; "the push that follows gives
  the version back"; and "calls that took access away are made again from the device's own list".
  §6.7 has hello, pull, push, pull; the calls of the list have no place in it. `access_removal` is
  device-local (§7.1). C18: of the calls the function log names, the operator repeats
  `delete_account`.
- Sequence. Ines owns the family; Tom is a member. Her devices are A and B.
  1. A backup is taken. On A, Ines removes Tom (`mark` 80). On B, she then shares the folder `f9`
     with the family (revision 90). Tom has never been able to read `f9`.
  2. The database is restored. Tom is a member again.
  3. B syncs first. The share is a row of her profile above its line: B sends it again, the server
     creates it (C18 case 4, as S77 has it for a share). B has no list entry: the removal was made
     on A. `f9` appears on Tom's device.
  4. A syncs and removes Tom again. His device purges what it has had since step 3.
- With one device the same can happen, because D7 gives no order between the push and the calls.
  And with a share that Ines ended after the backup: the restored server shows it live, her device
  holds its tombstone and a snippet written after it; when the two go in different requests (200
  ops each, in the order of the oldest entry, D1), the snippet is readable by the group in between.
- Why medium: it needs a restore, and the design already tells the owner that a removed member is
  a member again for a time (§11.3, question 3). What it does not say is that this person then
  receives what was written or shared after the removal, from devices that act by themselves.
- Fix. (1) D7 and §6.7: in a new epoch the device first makes the calls of its list, then sends
  the deletes of shares it holds above the line, and only then the rest. (2) C18, the operator:
  the function log holds the ids of every account call; the script that repeats `delete_account`
  can repeat `remove_member`, `leave_group`, `withdraw_invitation`, a lowered role, `delete_group`,
  `revoke_device` and `sign_out_everywhere` as well, before the server opens. That closes step 3,
  which no rule on B can, and it does not wait for a person to sign in (K1). The list on the
  device stays as the net. (3) §11.3 row 2 and question 3 then say what is left.

### K3. A file is still readable by its hash — medium, by reading (the rest of N3)

- Rules: D16, "a pulled snippet whose body names an attachment of its profile that the device
  holds without a live link gets the link from the first device that may write the snippet (a
  local change like any other)". A body names a file as `attachment:<sha256>`
  (`crates/iamb-store/src/attachments.rs:10`). C19: a row is backed "when its creator could read
  the verified file … at the moment the row was created". §5.13: the file is readable when "a
  snippet they can read has a live link to a backed attachment row with its profile and hash".
- Sequence, in the state of S73. Ines's private snippet uses a file with the hash H; her row for
  it is `at2`, backed.
  1. Tom saves the shared snippet `s1` with a body that contains `attachment:H`. One op, allowed.
     He writes no attachment row and no link, and uploads nothing.
  2. Ines's device pulls `s1`. Its body names an attachment of its profile that the device holds,
     `at2`, and `s1` has no link to it. Under D16 her device adds the link and pushes it. Tom's
     device cannot be "the first device": it does not hold `at2`.
  3. A snippet Tom can read has a live link to a backed row with profile P and hash H. The file is
     his to download, and that it arrived tells him Ines holds it.
- Second way, with a restore. Tom's two rows of N3 (`at9`, not backed, and its link) are lost by
  a restore. Ines's device holds them, they are rows of her profile, and it sends them again. The
  server does not know `at9` and creates it (C18 case 4), with Ines as the caller: she can read
  the file, so the row is backed.
- G14 and the last sentence of §4.4 fail as worded. Medium for the reason N3 was: he must know the
  hash.
- Fix. D16: the device adds a link only where it holds a deleted link between that snippet and
  that attachment, which is what a version restored from a conflict needs; or only for a body its
  own user wrote. C19 and §6.3: a re-pushed attachment row says whether the sender's user made it,
  as `author: "self"` does for content, and is backed only then or by an upload. A scenario beside
  S73.

### K4. The purge on a null folder has no `except` — medium, by reading

- Rules: D3, "A null row for a folder ends that tree on the device … then the folder and
  everything in it is removed". D6: visibility entries are handled "after the page's rows"; "a
  folder that arrives as a null row before its `folder_revoked` is purged at that moment, and the
  entry then finds nothing left". C21: a tree that is revoked while part of it stays readable gets
  `folder_revoked` "with the parts that stay listed in `except`".
- Sequence. S72, with one more row: the folder `f1c` below `f1b` is shared with the family by
  itself, as in S65. Ines moves `f1b` out of the shared folder.
  1. Tom's feed gets the row `f1b`, delivered as null, and `folder_revoked` for `f1b` with `f1c`
     in `except`.
  2. His device applies the row first: `f1b` and everything in it go, `f1c` and its snippets
     included; an unsent change in `f1c` becomes a kept change.
  3. `folder_revoked` finds nothing. No `folder_visible` follows, because `f1c` never stopped
     being readable.
- End: the server shows Tom `f1c`; none of his devices holds it, and nothing fetches it until a
  resync for another reason. I1 does not hold. The access check purges and does not fetch.
- Fix: the follow-up's second sentence for N7 instead of, or beside, the first. §5.5: a user for
  whom a transaction revokes a tree gets no `row` entries for rows inside it, so that the entry
  with its `except` is the only word on that tree. Or the null row is delivered with the same
  `except`. The scenario above.

### K5. A revoked session is still a session at the authentication service — medium; the password looked up, the rest unconfirmed

- Rules: §11.3 row 4, "The device row is marked revoked, and because every call and every policy
  checks it, access stops with that commit … the session cannot register another device". C25:
  "nothing depends on the authentication service ending the session". §11.6 has task 4.2 verify
  four facts about sessions, all about `auth.sessions`.
- The gap. The checks are in Iamb's functions and policies. The revoked session's tokens are
  still good at the authentication service, which knows nothing of device rows. Looked up,
  passwords guide: a signed-in user sets a new password with `updateUser`, and passing the
  current one is optional. Sign-in is by magic link or an identity provider (§3.1), so no user
  has a password; one can be set all the same. Whoever holds the lost computer's tokens sets a
  password, signs in with it, and calls `hello` as a new session with a new device id.

## 6. What else was read, without a finding

- **The line per profile.** A transaction that writes two profiles, one that locks a profile and
  writes no row of it, one that draws revisions and aborts, an account call that writes no synced
  row, the daily job: in each, every revision of a profile at or below its `head_rev` is in a
  consistent copy and every one above is not. It rests on a sequence without a cache and on the
  lock order, which §11.6 has task 4.2 verify, and that step is the right one.
- **Deletes and a restore.** A lost create and a lost delete of one row, in both orders of the two
  devices: the tombstone wins, because a re-push creates "a tombstone as a tombstone" and the
  higher lost revision is the later one. A re-pushed delete of a folder against a row that had
  been moved out in the lost time: the move comes back as a stale write with a conflict. A delete
  whose tombstone every device has purged is place 12, as §1 says.
- **Review and a restore.** A member's lost content given back by the owner's device gets the
  author "nobody" and is held; given back by the member's device it is the member's; an owner's
  label change on top of it (`repush: "base"`) does not make it the owner's. No order makes a
  member's content count as the reader's.
- **Positions.** A device row without a pair accepts one snapshot with any position and reads only
  its epoch; two installations with one id after `begin_epoch`, or both below the floor, are still
  caught at the next call of the one that did not snapshot last. A data directory put back from a
  backup on the same computer is signed out and asked to sign in again; C25 says so.
- **`server_behind`.** Only the claimant stops. An honest device behind a server that was restored
  without the procedure also stops its access check, which D12 states and gives the reason for.
- **Documents.** The lines the revision changed in ADR-0027 (points 8 and 9), ADR-0028 (points 2
  and 7), the architecture (§6.2, §7, §9) and the test strategy say what the design says. They
  repeat what K1 to K5 question and change with them; ADR-0027's "nothing a user made disappears
  without being on the server, in a conflict, or in a list on the device that made it" is K1.

### What is still not checked

Nothing was run against Postgres or Supabase. K1's fix does not depend on what the authentication
service does with a token issued after the backup, and 4.2's restore test finds out. K5's fix
rests on one fact about the platform that the documentation states with a reservation: that an
access token says how its session was opened (`amr` is listed as optional). It is the first thing
4.2 verifies, with the fallback named in §11.6. That the hook exists on the project's plan, what
the settings of §11.3 are by default, and whether the function log outlives the database with a
time of its own are verification steps as well; the restore script depends on the last. K2's
replay rests on that log holding every call that took something away, which is why the device's
own list stays. The scenarios S78 to S84 are right by reading and by the check as far as it looks.
The other 77 were not read again, except where a finding touches them (S49, S58, S66, S72, S73,
S74, S77), and all 84 pass the check, the new rules included.
