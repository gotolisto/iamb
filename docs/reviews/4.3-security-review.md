Date: 2026-11-21 · Commit reviewed: `9a2a300` (the merge `635d24c` has the same tree) · Reviewer:
independent, did not write the schema and had no access to its authors' reasoning

## 1. Verdict

The plain cases hold. A stranger, an outsider with a group of her own and a removed member get
no row through any function when they ask for it directly: 150 calls with the victim's ids
returned no content and changed nothing, locally and on the hosted project (what the answers
still give away is finding 10). A member who may only use a folder reads that folder and nothing
else. No schema but `api` and `api_service` is reachable through the REST interface; the live
channel and the storage bucket refuse another user's topic and files; no way was found around the
token hook or around a committed revocation.

The KPI "0 successful cross-account accesses" is still not met. Three findings let one account
read or change another's data, and each was carried out:

1. A member who may change one shared folder read an old version of a snippet that was written
   while the snippet was in the owner's private folder (finding 1). It needs no changed client:
   an ordinary offline edit is enough.
2. A removed member went on learning of conflicts in the folder he was removed from, and closed
   one for everybody (finding 2).
3. A member who knows the hash of a file in the owner's private library made that file readable
   for himself, with two requests sent at the same moment (finding 3). The same two requests
   leave a top-level folder in the owner's personal profile and access entries that no longer
   equal the rule of §4.2.

Two more findings let a member act beyond the role inside a group: moving a folder of the group
to the top level through "Restore this version" (finding 4, high), and taking over a group with
an offer of ownership that the owner thought ended when she removed him (finding 5, medium).

Nine further findings are medium: one account can stall another's writes, keep the retention job
from other people's deleted rows, block an owner's attachments, and stop the operator's restore
script; the rate limiter does not count requests that fail; several answers tell a removed member
what goes on in the group he left. One of the nine is not a weakness but stops everything: **on
the hosted project no writing call works through the REST interface** (`hello`, `push`, every
account call, `revoke_device` answer HTTP 400), because the platform rejects a `DELETE` without a
`WHERE` clause in the sessions that interface opens. The hosted tests of task 4.2 never ran a
writing call through that interface, so nothing had shown it (finding 14).

Findings 1, 2 and 4 follow the words of rules C3 and C20; the design itself contradicts §4.4, §4.5,
§11.3 and G13 there, and must change before the functions can.

The order I would take them in: finding 14 first, with finding 16's fix, because until then
nothing can be tested through the interface devices use; then finding 3, which is a change to the
code with no question for the design; then the design change behind findings 1, 2 and 4; then
findings 7 and 6 together.

## 3. Method

### 3.1 Locally

`server/tests/run.sh` was run first (21 of 21). The attacks ran against a second throwaway cluster
built the same way (Postgres 16, the stand-in as superuser, the ten migrations as `postgres`,
`tests/lib.sql`), one fresh database per attack, each statement as a distinct `authenticated`
user with the claims `iamb_test.act` sets. Attempted:

- **Direct access.** The privileges of `anon`, `authenticated` and `service_role` on every table,
  view, sequence and function, from the catalog and through the server's own grant tests; every
  synced table read as each actor after the steps of the random walk; another account's device id
  and position given to `enter` and `position`.
- **Every function of `api`** as a stranger, as a removed member and as an outsider with a group
  of her own: other accounts' device, profile, group, folder, row, share, invitation and conflict
  ids; positions and devices of others; a push of 37 ops (puts, moves, deletes with cascade lists
  naming the victim's rows, shares in both directions, links to private tags and files, `profile`
  and `membership` ops, a re-push); malformed and oversized arguments. Then, as a member who may
  change a folder, as one who may only use it and as an admin, the calls at the edge of each role
  (the items below).
- **Claims.** 28 shapes of `amr`, `session_id`, `sub` and `role`.
- **Conflicts and history**: who becomes a party, what `get_conflict` returns, who may close and
  restore, base versions from before a share.
- **Invitations and ownership**: other people's invitations, an unconfirmed address, repeated
  calls after a removal, offers across a removal.
- **Races**: two requests of one user that name the same new id in two profiles; a request that
  names a profile it may not write, against the owner's own request.
- **Attachments and storage**: rows and links with a private file's hash, upload reservations,
  the object policies by name.
- **The retention job and the restore script**: who decides the order of the job's work; what the
  script's parser makes of log lines a caller can cause.
- **Resource use**: the rate limiter against failing requests, the cascade list, the size of
  answers.
- **A random walk** for G1 (the KPI row above).

### 3.2 On the hosted project

Only project `gemyckdldyzcvrvwuivk`, through the Management API (the token is added by the network
proxy; no key or token was printed or stored) and through the project's public endpoints. Three
kinds of run:

1. **Read-only**: the REST, authentication and realtime settings (through an allow-list of
   fields), the catalog (grants, policies, functions, roles, publications, extensions, the cron
   job), the security advisor, and a few queries of the log.
2. **Transactions that were rolled back**, as `tests/hosted.sh` does: findings 1, 2, 4, 5, 12 and
   15, the sweep of 150 calls, and the storage and live-channel policies on the platform's own
   `storage.objects` and `realtime.messages`.
3. **Throwaway accounts with real sessions** (13 accounts, all removed again). The token hook
   refuses a password, and task 4.2 had no mailbox, so its probe never held a session Iamb
   accepts. This review opened such sessions without a mailbox: the account is a row of
   `auth.users` made by SQL; `recovery_token` is set to the SHA-224 of the address followed by a
   code of the script's choosing; `POST /auth/v1/verify` with `{"type": "magiclink", "email",
   "token"}` then opens a session at the authentication service itself. With these tokens: the
   REST interface (which schemas answer, every reading function as a stranger, as a member and
   after removal, every kind of writing call), the realtime service over a WebSocket (own topic,
   another user's topic as a private and as a public channel, without a token, sending as a
   client, a poke from the database, a socket across a revocation), the storage service (reads,
   lists, uploads and signed addresses for other people's names; no object was created), and the
   authentication service (password sign-in, anonymous sign-in, setting a password, a phone
   number, linking an identity, a second factor, sign-out with the scopes `others` and `local`
   against `enter`).
   Finding 3 was repeated there with two committed requests in parallel.

Nothing destructive was tried on the hosted project: findings 6 to 9 (stalling, exhausting,
starving) ran locally only, and no project setting was changed. §9 lists what the runs left.

### 3.3 Running the reproductions

Each script below runs with `psql` as `postgres` in a fresh copy of a database built like the
tests' template (as a user other than root, from the repository root):

    B=/usr/lib/postgresql/16/bin; W=$(mktemp -d); S=server; export PGHOST=$W PGPORT=54399
    $B/initdb -D $W/d -U supabase_admin --auth=trust -E UTF8 --locale=C >/dev/null
    $B/pg_ctl -D $W/d -o "-k $W -c listen_addresses= -p 54399" -l $W/log -w start
    $B/psql -U supabase_admin -d postgres -c "create database t"
    $B/psql -U supabase_admin -d t -f $S/tests/stub/supabase.sql
    for m in $S/supabase/migrations/*.sql; do $B/psql -U postgres -d t -f $m; done
    $B/psql -U postgres -d t -f $S/tests/lib.sql
    $B/psql -U supabase_admin -d postgres -c "create database a1 template t"   # one per script
    $B/psql -U postgres -d a1                                                    # paste the script

The scripts use the tests' own helpers: `iamb_test.family('edit')` makes Ines (owner of a family,
with the folders `f1`, shared with the family to change, and `f2`, private, and a snippet in each),
Tom (member) and a stranger; `iamb_test.push`, `pull` and `call` act as the named user with the
role `authenticated`. Results are the `NOTICE` lines quoted under each script.

### 1. A conflict hands old versions to a newcomer — high, demonstrated locally and on the hosted project

- Where: `record_conflict` copies every version of the row's open conflict into the new one and
  makes their authors and the new writer its parties (`…100500_api_push.sql:233-243`);
  `get_conflict` returns each version's history row to any party who can read the row now
  (`…100600_api_read.sql:372-398`). It does not ask where the row was, or whether the reader had
  access, when that version was written.
- Rule: §4.4 "Not readable: the owner's other folders and their snippets … the history of any
  row"; §4.5 "History rows of `P`'s rows: none" (G2). Against them C3: "the new one takes over
  every version the old one offered, with their authors as parties". G5 is kept to the letter:
  the reader is a party and can read the row now; C3 is what makes him a party.
- What: an owner with two computers has an open conflict on a snippet in a private folder. Later
  she moves the snippet into a shared folder, or shares the folder. The first stale write by a
  member records a conflict that takes the old versions over, and the member's conflict view
  shows them. No changed client is needed: in the second variant below the member's device sends
  the base it really holds.
- Reproduction (local; the same script ran on the hosted project inside `begin … rollback` with
  the same result):

  ```sql
  do $$
  declare
      ids jsonb := iamb_test.family('edit');   -- f1 is shared to change, f2 is private
      f1 uuid := (ids ->> 'f1')::uuid; f2 uuid := (ids ->> 'f2')::uuid; s2 uuid := (ids ->> 's2')::uuid;
      r1 bigint; r jsonb; c uuid;
  begin
      perform iamb_test.new_device('ines2', 'ines'); perform iamb_test.hello('ines2');   -- her second computer
      r1 := iamb_test.rev('snippet', s2);
      -- s2 is in the private folder f2. Her two computers disagree about it; the conflict stays open.
      perform iamb_test.push('ines',  jsonb_build_array(iamb_test.op_snippet(s2, f2, ';priv', 'PIN of the safe: 4711', r1)));
      perform iamb_test.push('ines2', jsonb_build_array(iamb_test.op_snippet(s2, f2, ';priv', 'Nothing to see', r1)));
      -- She moves the snippet, now harmless, into the shared folder f1.
      perform iamb_test.push('ines2', jsonb_build_array(iamb_test.op_snippet(s2, f1, ';priv', 'Nothing to see', iamb_test.rev('snippet', s2))));
      -- Tom (edit on f1, no access entry for f2) sends a stale edit of s2 and reads the conflict it records.
      perform iamb_test.pull('tom');
      r := iamb_test.push('tom', jsonb_build_array(iamb_test.op_snippet(s2, f1, ';priv', 'Tom was here', 0)));
      c := (r -> 'results' -> 0 ->> 'conflict')::uuid;
      r := iamb_test.call('tom', format('api.get_conflict(iamb_test.dev(''tom''), 1, %L)', c));
      raise notice 'Tom has % access entries for f2; get_conflict offers him: %',
          (select count(*) from iamb.folder_access where user_id = iamb_test.uid('tom') and folder_id = f2),
          (select string_agg(format('%L (in f2: %s)', v -> 'row' ->> 'body', (v -> 'row' ->> 'folder_id') = f2::text), ', ' order by v ->> 'rev')
           from jsonb_array_elements(r -> 'versions') v);
  end $$;
  ```

  Result: `Tom has 0 access entries for f2; get_conflict offers him: 'PIN of the safe: 4711' (in
  f2: t), 'Nothing to see' (in f2: f)`. The stranger asking for the same conflict gets
  `not_found`.
- Second variant, shown locally: no move. The owner's conflict is from before she shared the
  folder; the member's device holds the current revision, the owner edits once more, the member's
  offline edit arrives with the base it holds. His conflict offers `'my bank: IBAN XX00 1234'`,
  written before the folder was shared.
- Fix: in the design first. A taken-over version should go to the parties of the conflict it came
  from; the new writer becomes party to the version his write replaced. `get_conflict` (and the
  restore of finding 4) should return a version only when the reader's access covers the folder
  the version was in and began before the version was replaced (`granted_at` against
  `replaced_at`). S57 keeps working: its three people all had the folder throughout.

### 2. A removed member still learns of conflicts and can close them — high, demonstrated locally and on the hosted project

- Where: the policy on `conflict` asks only "is the caller in `parties`"
  (`…100300_policies.sql:126-133`), `conflicts()` reads through it (`…100600_api_read.sql:347-358`),
  `record_conflict` writes a feed entry for every party of the old and the new conflict
  (`…100500_api_push.sql:243`), and `acct_resolve_conflict` closes on "keep" after the party check
  alone (`…100600_api_read.sql:422-433`). `get_conflict` and "restore" do check the right to read
  and write, and refuse.
- Rule: §11.3 "A removed member … Nothing new from the moment the removal commits: every policy
  and every function refuses them"; G13. Against them C20: "Any party may close a conflict."
- What: a member who once wrote a row stays a party of its conflicts after he is removed. A later
  conflict between the remaining people takes his version over, so he is a party of that one too:
  his feed gets the entry (row id, conflict id), `conflicts()` lists it (kind, state, time, the
  winner's revision), and `resolve_conflict(…, 'keep')` closes it. After that the others can no
  longer restore the version it offered; the history row waits for the retention job.
- Reproduction (local; on the hosted project inside `begin … rollback` with the same result):

  ```sql
  do $$
  declare
      ids jsonb := iamb_test.family('edit');
      f1 uuid := (ids ->> 'f1')::uuid; s1 uuid := (ids ->> 's1')::uuid; g uuid := (ids ->> 'g')::uuid;
      base bigint; r jsonb; c uuid;
  begin
      perform iamb_test.new_device('ines2', 'ines'); perform iamb_test.hello('ines2');
      perform iamb_test.pull('tom');
      -- Tom writes s1 once while he is a member (so that a later conflict takes his version over).
      base := iamb_test.rev('snippet', s1);
      perform iamb_test.push('tom',  jsonb_build_array(iamb_test.op_snippet(s1, f1, ';hrs', 'Tom''s hours', base)));
      perform iamb_test.push('ines', jsonb_build_array(iamb_test.op_snippet(s1, f1, ';hrs', 'Ines''s hours', base)));
      -- Ines removes Tom. From here on he has no access entry in her profile.
      perform iamb_test.call('ines', format('api.remove_member(iamb_test.dev(''ines''), 1, %L, %L)', g, iamb_test.uid('tom')));
      perform iamb_test.pull('tom');
      -- After the removal her two computers conflict on s1.
      base := iamb_test.rev('snippet', s1);
      perform iamb_test.push('ines',  jsonb_build_array(iamb_test.op_snippet(s1, f1, ';hrs', 'After Tom left, v1', base)));
      r := iamb_test.push('ines2', jsonb_build_array(iamb_test.op_snippet(s1, f1, ';hrs', 'After Tom left, v2', base)));
      c := (r -> 'results' -> 0 ->> 'conflict')::uuid;
      raise notice 'feed of the removed member: %', (select jsonb_agg(e - 'seq') from jsonb_array_elements(iamb_test.pull('tom') -> 'entries') e);
      raise notice 'conflicts() as the removed member: %', iamb_test.call('tom', 'api.conflicts(iamb_test.dev(''tom''), 1)') -> 'conflicts';
      raise notice 'get_conflict: %', iamb_test.call('tom', format('api.get_conflict(iamb_test.dev(''tom''), 1, %L)', c)) ->> 'error';
      raise notice 'resolve_conflict keep, as the removed member: %', iamb_test.call('tom', format('api.resolve_conflict(iamb_test.dev(''tom''), 1, %L, ''keep'')', c)) - 'epoch';
      raise notice 'state: %, resolved by Tom: %', (select state from iamb.conflict where id = c), (select resolved_by = iamb_test.uid('tom') from iamb.conflict where id = c);
      raise notice 'Ines restores the version it offered: %', iamb_test.call('ines', format('api.resolve_conflict(iamb_test.dev(''ines''), 1, %L, ''restore'', %s)', c,
          (select max(rev) from iamb.conflict_version where conflict_id = c))) - 'epoch';
      raise notice 'Ines''s row is still: %', (select body from iamb.snippet where id = s1);
  end $$;
  ```

  Result: the feed holds `{"kind": "conflict", "entity": "snippet", "id": …, "conflict": …}`;
  `conflicts()` lists the new conflict (`edit_edit`, `open`, its time and `winner_rev`);
  `get_conflict` answers `forbidden`; `resolve_conflict … keep` answers `{"error": null, "state":
  "resolved"}`; the row says `resolved`, by Tom; Ines's restore answers `state: resolved` and
  changes nothing, her row is still `After Tom left, v2`.
- `tests/sql/15_conflicts.sql` expects that a member whose share was lowered to "use only" may
  close a conflict; that follows C20. The removed member is the case §11.3 excludes.
- Fix: in `conflicts`, in `resolve_conflict` (also for "keep") and before the feed entry, require
  what `get_conflict` requires: the caller can read the row. Better, when access to a profile ends,
  take the user out of `parties` of that profile's conflicts in the same transaction, and do not
  carry a version's author into a new conflict when that author can no longer read the row.

### 3. Two requests that create one id in two profiles — high, demonstrated locally and on the hosted project

- Where: `store_row` writes every content row with `insert … on conflict (id) do update`
  (`…100200_private_helpers.sql:820-875`). `push_op` decides "this is a create" from a read made
  under the lock of the profile the op names (`…100500_api_push.sql:529`, `:895`). An id is unique
  across all profiles, and the lock of C24 is per profile: two requests that name the same new id
  in two profiles hold different locks. The second one's insert waits for the first one's commit
  and then updates the first one's row with its own columns, without any of the checks an update
  of that row would get. For an attachment the update copies `backed` (`:869`); for a folder,
  `parent_id` and `member_permission` (`:830-832`); for a snippet, `folder_id` and the content
  (`:846-849`).
- Rule: G14 ("a row becomes backed only by the server's hand … a request for any other name, also
  for an existing file whose hash the user knows … answers exactly like one for a file that does
  not exist"), G1, G7, and C24: "One writer per profile at a time" is the whole argument for
  these. (The other order of the same two folder requests would leave a folder whose parent is in
  another profile, against §5.2; that order was not run.)
- What a member with `edit` on one folder of the owner's personal profile gets from it:
  - *The owner's private file.* Request 1 creates attachment row `A` in the owner's profile with
    the hash of her private file (allowed; not backed). Request 2, from his other device, creates
    "the same" row `A` in his own profile with the hash of a file of his own, for which the server
    computes `backed = true`. Row `A` ends in the owner's profile, with her file's hash, backed.
    He links it to a shared snippet and the storage policy gives him the file. He must know the
    hash, which is the case S73 and G14 are written for.
  - *A top-level folder in the owner's personal profile* (a push answers `rejected: forbidden`),
    while his access entry for it stays: `folder_access` gives him `edit` on a folder that, by
    §4.2, only the owner reads. Whatever the owner puts into the folder that appeared at the top
    of her library he can read until something else recomputes her entries.
- The window is not narrow. The second request does not have to arrive in an instant: it waits
  for the first to commit. The first only has to be still running, and finding 7 lets its sender
  choose how long it runs.
- The file variant needs one verified file of the member's own in his own profile, which a device
  can have only once the upload commit of task 4.6 exists (the reproduction inserts the two
  `attachment_blob` rows as that commit would). The folder variant needs nothing.
- Reproduction (local, two `psql` sessions on one database):

  ```sql
  -- setup, session 1
  select iamb_test.put('ids', iamb_test.family('edit'));
  select iamb_test.new_device('tom2', 'tom'); select iamb_test.hello('tom2'); select iamb_test.pull('tom');
  -- Ines's private verified file (hash a1…) and a verified file of Tom's own in his own profile (70…),
  -- as the commit of an upload leaves them.
  insert into iamb.attachment_blob values ((iamb_test.get('ids') ->> 'p_ines')::uuid, repeat('a1', 32), 1000, 'image/png', 1),
                                          ((iamb_test.get('ids') ->> 'p_tom')::uuid, repeat('70', 32), 10, 'image/png', 1);
  insert into storage.objects (bucket_id, name) values ('attachments', (iamb_test.get('ids') ->> 'p_ines') || '/' || repeat('a1', 32));
  select iamb_test.put('A', to_jsonb(gen_random_uuid()));

  -- session 1: Tom's first device. Row A in INES's profile, naming her file. Leave the transaction open.
  begin;
  select iamb_test.push('tom', jsonb_build_array(iamb_test.op_attachment((iamb_test.get('A') #>> '{}')::uuid,
         (iamb_test.get('ids') ->> 'p_ines')::uuid, repeat('a1', 32)))) -> 'results' -> 0;     -- applied, "blob": "missing"

  -- session 2: Tom's second device. "The same" row A in his OWN profile, naming his own file. It waits.
  select iamb_test.push('tom2', jsonb_build_array(iamb_test.op_attachment((iamb_test.get('A') #>> '{}')::uuid,
         (iamb_test.get('ids') ->> 'p_tom')::uuid, repeat('70', 32)))) -> 'results' -> 0 ->> 'status';

  -- session 1; run what follows the commit once session 2 has answered (applied)
  commit;
  select profile_id = (iamb_test.get('ids') ->> 'p_ines')::uuid as in_ines_profile, left(sha256, 4) as sha, backed
  from iamb.attachment where id = (iamb_test.get('A') #>> '{}')::uuid;                          -- t | a1a1 | t
  select iamb_test.pull('tom');
  select iamb_test.st(iamb_test.push('tom', jsonb_build_array(iamb_test.op_snippet_attachment(gen_random_uuid(),
         (iamb_test.get('ids') ->> 's1')::uuid, (iamb_test.get('A') #>> '{}')::uuid))));       -- applied
  select iamb_test.act('tom');
  select right(name, 64) = repeat('a1', 32) as tom_reads_the_private_file from storage.objects;  -- t (no row before)
  select count(*) from iamb.attachment_blob;                                                     -- 2 (1 before)
  select iamb_test.act_owner();
  ```

  As single requests, without an open transaction: request 1 carried, after the attachment op, a
  delete of Ines's private folder with a cascade list of 9,000 unknown ids (refused, but answered,
  finding 7), which kept it open for 10.7 s; request 2 was sent 1.5 s later and answered after
  9.2 s; the result was the same.
- The folder variant, shown locally the same way (request 1: a new folder `X` under the shared
  folder; request 2: `X` at the top level of Tom's own profile): `X` is in Ines's profile, parent
  none, written by Tom; the entries for `X` are `ines = manage/own` and `tom = edit/share`, and
  `iamb_test.check_access()` fails with "folder_access differs from the reference in 3 rows".
- On the hosted project, with two throwaway accounts and committed requests (request 1 with a list
  of 4,000 ids ran 4.0 s; request 2, sent 1 s later, answered `applied` after 2.9 s): row `A` in
  the owner's profile, `sha` `a1a1…`, `backed = true`, written by the member; through the REST
  interface with the member's own token `fetch_folder` lists the attachment with `"verified":
  true`, and `attachment_upload_begin` for that hash answers `exists` where it had opened an
  upload before. No storage object was made there (a row of `storage.objects` cannot be removed
  by SQL); the storage policy is the same condition as the one `fetch_folder` reports, and was
  shown on the platform's table in a rolled-back transaction (§5).
- Fix: when `push_op` has found no row, `store_row` must insert and nothing else (`on conflict do
  nothing`, and an op whose insert wrote no row is answered `blocked: invalid`), never update. Take
  a lock on the id (an advisory lock on entity and id, after the profile locks) before reading the
  row, so that the read and the write are one step whatever profile the op names. Independently,
  the `do update` lists should not carry `backed`, and an update should name the profile it
  expects. Add this case to `20_concurrency.sql`.

### 4. "Restore this version" moves a folder where a push may not — high, demonstrated locally and on the hosted project

- Where: `acct_resolve_conflict` checks, for a folder, `manage` in the profile when the folder is
  at the top now, else `edit` on its current parent (`…100600_api_read.sql:449-452`). It then
  writes the version's parent, name, defaults and `member_permission`
  (`:474-479`, `:490-496`). For a snippet it checks `edit` on both folders but not that they lie in
  one access root.
- Rule: §4.3 (a move to the top, a folder at the top and `member_permission` need `manage`; a
  move out of the access root needs `manage`); §11.3 "They cannot … move content out of the tree
  … or change who sees it"; G7.
- What: a member with `edit` restores a version that puts a folder of the group at the top level,
  with the member permission that version had. In a personal profile the same call moves a folder
  out of the shared tree, or under another shared root, which changes who can read it. The version
  must be on offer to him, which finding 1 arranges.
- Reproduction (local; on the hosted project inside `begin … rollback` with the same result):

  ```sql
  do $$
  declare
      ids jsonb := iamb_test.family('edit'); g uuid := (ids ->> 'g')::uuid;
      t1 uuid := gen_random_uuid(); x uuid := gen_random_uuid(); r1 bigint; r jsonb; c uuid;
  begin
      perform iamb_test.new_device('ines2', 'ines'); perform iamb_test.hello('ines2');
      -- Two folders of the group, both at the top: T1 (members may change it) and X.
      perform iamb_test.push('ines', jsonb_build_array(
          iamb_test.op_folder(t1, g, null, 'T1', 0, '{"row": {"member_permission": "edit"}}'),
          iamb_test.op_folder(x,  g, null, 'X',  0, '{"row": {"member_permission": "edit"}}')));
      perform iamb_test.pull('ines2'); perform iamb_test.pull('tom');
      r1 := iamb_test.rev('folder', x);
      -- The owner renames X on one computer and moves it under T1 on the other: her own open conflict.
      perform iamb_test.push('ines',  jsonb_build_array(iamb_test.op_folder(x, g, null, 'X renamed', r1, '{"row": {"member_permission": "edit"}}')));
      perform iamb_test.push('ines2', jsonb_build_array(iamb_test.op_folder(x, g, t1, 'X under T1', r1)));
      perform iamb_test.pull('tom');
      raise notice 'Tom (member) moves X to the top by push: %',
          iamb_test.st(iamb_test.push('tom', jsonb_build_array(iamb_test.op_folder(x, g, null, 'X', iamb_test.rev('folder', x)))));
      -- A stale rename by Tom takes the owner's versions over; he restores the one from the top level.
      r := iamb_test.push('tom', jsonb_build_array(iamb_test.op_folder(x, g, t1, 'X renamed by Tom', 0)));
      c := (r -> 'results' -> 0 ->> 'conflict')::uuid;
      raise notice 'restore by Tom: %', iamb_test.call('tom', format('api.resolve_conflict(iamb_test.dev(''tom''), 1, %L, ''restore'', %s)', c,
          (select min(rev) from iamb.conflict_version where conflict_id = c))) - 'epoch';
      raise notice 'X now: top level %, member_permission %, written by Tom %', (select parent_id is null from iamb.folder where id = x),
          (select member_permission from iamb.folder where id = x), (select updated_by = iamb_test.uid('tom') from iamb.folder where id = x);
  end $$;
  ```

  Result: `Tom (member) moves X to the top by push: rejected:forbidden`; `restore by Tom: {"error":
  null, "state": "resolved"}`; `X now: top level t, member_permission edit, written by Tom t`.
- Fix: build the row the restore would write and judge it with the checks of `push_op` for a put
  of that row by that user (target parent, top level, share roots, access roots, depth). One code
  path for both is the safest form.

### 5. An offer of ownership outlives the membership it was made to — medium, demonstrated locally and on the hosted project

- Where: `end_membership` ends shares and access and leaves `profile.offered_owner`
  (`…100400_api_account.sql:784-810`); `acct_accept_ownership` asks only that the caller is the
  person named and a live member now (`:482-485`).
- Rule: C16 "`accept_ownership`, by the member it names, needs that member to be a live member
  still"; §4.1: an admin may not hand the group over.
- What: the owner offers the group to a member, thinks better of it and removes him. Within the
  14 days of the offer any admin can invite him back as a plain member; he accepts the old offer,
  is the owner, and removes the former owner. It needs an admin's invitation, hence medium.
- Reproduction (local; on the hosted project inside `begin … rollback` with the same result):

  ```sql
  do $$

      ids jsonb := iamb_test.family('edit'); g uuid := (ids ->> 'g')::uuid; i1 uuid := gen_random_uuid(); i2 uuid := gen_random_uuid();

      perform iamb_test.call('tom', format('api.accept_invitation(iamb_test.dev(''tom''), 1, %L)', i2));
  ```

  Result: `removed, Tom accepts: no_offer`; `back as a member, Tom accepts: {"mark": 22, "error":

- Fix: `end_membership` clears `offered_owner` and `offered_at` when the membership it ends is the
  one the offer names (and tells both feeds, as a withdrawn offer does).

### 6. A request locks profiles it has no right in — medium, demonstrated locally

- Where: `push_core` locks every profile `op_profile` returns (`…100500_api_push.sql:1105-1108`),
  and `op_profile` takes the profile from the op's own row when the server does not know the row
  (`…100900_push_profile_fix.sql:33-35`): a tag or attachment put names any `profile_id`, a snippet
  put any folder. The rights are checked afterwards. `attachment_upload_begin` and
  `withdraw_invitation` lock before their check as well (`…100600_api_read.sql:533`,
  `…100400_api_account.sql:621`).
- What: whoever knows a profile's id holds that profile's lock for as long as his request runs:
  up to push's 20 s, and with finding 7 he chooses how long and is not counted. Every write of
  that profile waits: its owner's pushes, its members', account calls. On the hosted project the
  REST role has a lock time-out of 8 s, so a waiting request fails after 8 s. A group's id is
  known to every member, former member and invited person; a personal profile's id to everyone it
  ever shared a folder with.
- Reproduction (local, two `psql` sessions on one database):

  ```sql
  -- setup
  select iamb_test.put('ids', iamb_test.family('edit')); select iamb_test.pull('stranger'); select iamb_test.pull('ines');
  -- session 1: the stranger (no group, no share; he knows only the id of Ines's personal profile)
  begin;
  select iamb_test.st(iamb_test.push('stranger', jsonb_build_array(
      iamb_test.op_tag(gen_random_uuid(), (iamb_test.get('ids') ->> 'p_ines')::uuid, 'x'))));   -- rejected:forbidden
  -- session 2: Ines saves a snippet in her own private folder. It waits until session 1 ends.
  select iamb_test.st(iamb_test.push('ines', jsonb_build_array(iamb_test.op_snippet((iamb_test.get('ids') ->> 's2')::uuid,
      (iamb_test.get('ids') ->> 'f2')::uuid, ';priv', 'edited', iamb_test.rev('snippet', (iamb_test.get('ids') ->> 's2')::uuid)))));
  -- session 1
  commit;
  ```

  As one request, without an open transaction: the stranger's push held three ops (a tag put
  naming Ines's personal profile, one naming her group, and a delete of a folder of his own with
  a cascade list of 10,000 unknown ids). It answered `rejected:forbidden / rejected:forbidden /
  unchanged` after 10.4 s. Ines's push, started 1 s into it, answered after 9.3 s; alone it takes
  58 ms. `pg_blocking_pids` named the stranger's request.
- Fix: before locking, drop every profile in which the caller has no access entry, membership or
  ownership, and answer its ops `rejected: parent_gone` (which also closes the first oracle of
  finding 10). `attachment_upload_begin` and `withdraw_invitation`: check first without the lock,
  then lock and check again.

### 7. Failing requests are not counted, and a cascade list is unbounded — medium, demonstrated locally

- Where: `enter` counts a call in the transaction of the call
  (`…100200_private_helpers.sql:202-229`). §6.1 makes refusals data "so that what the function
  already wrote (a rate counter …) is committed", but a request that ends in an SQL error or in the
  statement time-out rolls the counter back with everything else. `push_core` bounds a request at
  200 ops and 4 MiB (`…100500_api_push.sql:1054`); §6.3's bound for a cascade list (20,000 rows) is
  not there, and `cascade_answer` appends to a JSON array row by row
  (`:346-373`), which copies the array each time. §6.3's "the answer stays within the 4 MiB of a
  request … in the short form with `fetch`" is not implemented either. The list is answered also
  when the delete is refused (`:422-423`) or changes nothing (`:407`, `:426`).
- What: a request of 1 MB keeps a database process busy for the whole 20 s; one of a few kB
  makes it build an answer of hundreds of megabytes; and requests that end in the time-out, or in
  an error the sender plants in the last op (`"base_rev": "x"`), are free. That is what makes
  findings 3 and 6 practical, and it is the lever for exhausting a small instance.
- Measured (local, 4 cores; no time-out applies in `psql`, on the platform push stops at 20 s):

  | Cascade list of a delete of an own, already deleted folder | Request | Time | Answer |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | 2,000 unknown ids | 138 kB | 0.4 s | 167 kB |
  | 5,000 | 345 kB | 3.2 s | 416 kB |
  | 10,000 | 690 kB | 11.1 s | 831 kB |
  | 20,000 (what §6.3 allows) | 1.4 MB | 41 s | 1.7 MB |
  | 60,000 (still under 4 MiB) | 4.1 MB | 390 s | 5.0 MB |
  | 100 entries naming the caller's own ten snippets of 400 kB | 8 kB | 2.9 s | 40 MB |
  | 500 such entries | 42 kB | 67 s; the process held 335 MB after 20 s and 2.25 GB at its peak | 200 MB |

  So a device that honestly deletes a folder with 20,000 rows below it cannot do so either.
- Reproduction (local):

  ```sql
  do $$
  declare
      ids jsonb := iamb_test.family('edit'); df uuid := gen_random_uuid(); r jsonb; t0 timestamptz; list jsonb; n integer;
  begin
      perform iamb_test.pull('stranger');
      -- (a) a call that ends in an SQL error is not counted
      raise notice 'push_calls counted before: %', coalesce((select count from iamb.rate_bucket where subject = iamb_test.dev('stranger') and bucket = 'push_calls'), 0);
      for i in 1..5 loop
          begin
              perform iamb_test.push('stranger', jsonb_build_array(jsonb_build_object('op_id', gen_random_uuid(), 'entity', 'tag', 'id', gen_random_uuid(), 'kind', 'delete', 'base_rev', 'x')));
          exception when invalid_text_representation then perform iamb_test.act_owner();
          end;
      end loop;
      raise notice 'after 5 pushes that failed (base_rev is not a number): %', coalesce((select count from iamb.rate_bucket where subject = iamb_test.dev('stranger') and bucket = 'push_calls'), 0);
      -- (b) what one accepted request can cost: the cascade list of a delete of a folder he deleted already
      perform iamb_test.push('stranger', jsonb_build_array(iamb_test.op_folder(df, iamb_test.personal('stranger'), null, 'd')));
      perform iamb_test.push('stranger', jsonb_build_array(iamb_test.op_delete('folder', df, 0)));
      foreach n in array array[2000, 5000, 10000] loop
          select jsonb_agg(jsonb_build_object('entity', 'snippet', 'id', gen_random_uuid())) into list from generate_series(1, n);
          t0 := clock_timestamp();
          r := iamb_test.push('stranger', jsonb_build_array(iamb_test.op_delete('folder', df, 0, jsonb_build_object('cascade', list))));
          raise notice '% ids in the list (request % kB): % after % s', n, octet_length(list::text) / 1000, iamb_test.st(r), round(extract(epoch from clock_timestamp() - t0)::numeric, 1);
      end loop;
  end $$;
  ```

  Result: `push_calls counted before: 0`, `after 5 pushes that failed …: 0`, then `unchanged after
  0.4 s`, `2.8 s`, `10.6 s`. On the hosted project, through the REST interface: five account calls
  that failed (finding 14) left the account's counter at 4, where it had been. That a request
  stopped by the time-out is not counted either follows from the same rollback and was not run.
- Fix: check the list's length (and that `base_rev`, `repush_rev`, `follows` and `cascade` have
  their types) in `push_core` before any lock, and answer `too_large` or `blocked: invalid` as
  data; build `cascade_answer` with one aggregate; implement the 4 MiB bound and the short form;
  do not answer the list of a delete that names a folder the caller cannot read. For the counter
  there is no clean fix inside one transaction: keep every refusal data, keep what a failing
  request can cost small, and put a limit in front of the database where the platform offers one.

### 8. Upload reservations — medium, demonstrated locally; the bytes by reading

- Where: `acct_attachment_upload_begin` counts every open upload of the profile against the
  profile's `storage_bytes_max`, whoever opened it, with the size the caller declares, for 24
  hours (`…100600_api_read.sql:544-553`); the policy on `attachment_upload` shows a row to its
  uploader only; no call ends another person's upload. The storage policy for the object does not
  look at a size (`…100300_policies.sql:274-281`); the bucket's limit is 25 MiB an object.
- What, three things:
  - A member who may change one folder of the owner's reserves all 500 MiB with 20 uploads he
    never sends (35 calls; 30 a minute are allowed). The owner's own uploads answer `plan_limit`
    for a day, he renews it daily, and she cannot see why. §5.10 accepts that a member uses up
    the owner's limit because "the owner sees and can delete what was added"; here she can do
    neither.
  - While he does it, `plan_limit` against sizes of his choosing tells him the total size of her
    verified files, private ones included, to the byte if he cares (in a run in which she held
    two files of 1,000 bytes he could reserve 524,286,000 of the 524,288,000 bytes and no more).
  - By reading: an upload declared with size 0 reserves nothing, and its object can still be 25
    MiB. 30 uploads a minute per account are 750 MiB a minute of objects that stay until the job
    of task 4.6 removes them. Not run on the hosted project, because an object made there could
    not have been removed with the means of this review.
- Reproduction (local):

  ```sql
  do $$
  declare
      ids jsonb := iamb_test.family('edit'); p_ines uuid := (ids ->> 'p_ines')::uuid; i integer := 0; r jsonb; sz bigint := 26214400; n integer := 0; total bigint := 0;
  begin
      -- Tom, who may change ONE folder of Ines's, opens uploads in her profile and never sends a byte.
      while sz >= 1024 loop
          i := i + 1;
          if i % 25 = 0 then delete from iamb.rate_bucket where subject = iamb_test.uid('tom'); end if;   -- a minute passes (30 account calls a minute)
          r := iamb_test.call('tom', format('api.attachment_upload_begin(iamb_test.dev(''tom''), 1, %L, %L, %s, ''image/png'')', p_ines, md5(i::text) || md5(i::text), sz));
  ```

  Result: `Tom: 35 calls, 20 uploads open, 524288000 bytes reserved for 24 hours`; `Ines, a file of

- Fix: a small bound on open uploads per uploader and profile; count an open upload against its
  uploader's own allowance until it is committed; show the profile's owner the open uploads and let
  her end them; refuse a declared size of 0; have the commit compare the object's real size, and
  bound the object at upload by its declared size where the storage service allows.

### 9. One account keeps the retention job from everybody else's rows — medium, demonstrated locally

- Where: `daily_job_step` walks the profiles "groups by id, then personal profiles by id" with one
  budget of `cascade_max` rows a run and stops when it is spent
  (`…100800_daily_job_steps.sql:120-141`); the id of a group is whatever its creator passes to
  `create_group` (`…100400_api_account.sql:266`). The expiry of invitations, offers and conflicts
  and the removal of unused attachments are inside the same loop.
- Rule: §5.12 (tombstones are purged 30 days after `deleted_at`), C22; correction 6.
- What: 2,000 rows every ten minutes are 200 a minute. An account that creates and deletes 200
  snippets a minute in a group with a low id (400 ops a minute; the limit is 6,000) has, 30 days
  on, 2,000 tombstones coming of age before every run. The job never gets past that group:
  everybody else's deleted rows, with their history, stay in the database for as long as the
  account goes on.
- Reproduction (local):

  ```sql
  do $$

      perform iamb_test.new_user('mallory'); perform iamb_test.hello('mallory', 'Mallory');
      -- Mallory's group gets the lowest id she can think of; the job works on groups in the order of their ids.
      perform iamb_test.call('mallory', 'api.create_group(iamb_test.dev(''mallory''), 1, ''00000000-0000-4000-8000-000000000000'', ''family'', ''first'')');
      perform iamb_test.push('mallory', jsonb_build_array(iamb_test.op_folder(mf, '00000000-0000-4000-8000-000000000000', null, 'churn')));
      for i in 1..20 loop       -- 4,000 snippets made and deleted through the API (20 seconds' worth of the push limits)
          delete from iamb.rate_bucket;
          batch := array(select gen_random_uuid() from generate_series(1, 200));
          perform iamb_test.push('mallory', (select jsonb_agg(iamb_test.op_snippet(x, mf, ';c', 'c')) from unnest(batch) x));
          perform iamb_test.push('mallory', (select jsonb_agg(iamb_test.op_delete('snippet', x, 0)) from unnest(batch) x));
      end loop;
      -- Ines deletes her private snippet. Then 31 days pass (the dates are moved back instead).
      perform iamb_test.push('ines', jsonb_build_array(iamb_test.op_delete('snippet', s2, iamb_test.rev('snippet', s2))));
      update iamb.snippet set deleted_at = deleted_at - 31 * 86400000::bigint where deleted_at is not null;
      r := iamb_private.daily_job_step();
      raise notice 'run 1: %; Ines''s deleted snippet still stored: %', r, (select body from iamb.snippet where id = s2);
      r := iamb_private.daily_job_step();
      raise notice 'run 2: %; still stored: %', r, exists (select 1 from iamb.snippet where id = s2);
      r := iamb_private.daily_job_step();

  ```

  Result: `run 1: {"feed": 0, "more": true, "rows": 2000, "devices": 0}; Ines's deleted snippet

  stopped: {… "more": false, "rows": 1 …}; still stored: f`. In a longer run with 2,000 more
  tombstones before each run the snippet was still there after four runs.
- Fix: give each profile a share of the budget (or remember where the last run stopped and go on
  from there), take the non-purge steps out of the budgeted loop, and write the number of rows
  left to the log so that a job that falls behind is seen.

### 12. The device to be revoked can keep the revocation from being made — medium, demonstrated locally and on the hosted project

- Where: `enter` counts `revoke_device` and `sign_out_everywhere` in the account's `account_calls`
  (30 a minute), with 17 other calls (`…100200_private_helpers.sql:204-219`). A call that is
  refused as data counts, so any signed-in device of the account can fill the counter with calls
  that do nothing.
- Rule: §11.3 "The owner of the account revokes the device from another device, or signs out
  everywhere … access stops with that commit"; G15.
- What: whoever holds a member's device, or a stolen session, sends 30 cheap calls at the start of
  every minute. The owner's `revoke_device` and `sign_out_everywhere` answer `rate_limited`. The
  owner has a chance at each turn of the minute and can still end all sessions at the

  ```sql

      end loop;
      raise notice 'the thief''s calls answer: %', r ->> 'error';
      raise notice 'revoke_device by the owner: %', iamb_test.call('owner', 'api.revoke_device(iamb_test.dev(''owner''), 1, iamb_test.dev(''thief''))') - 'epoch';
  ```

  Result: `the thief's calls answer: not_found`; `revoke_device by the owner: {"error":

### 13. The restore script stops on a line any account can write — medium, parser shown locally, the log's shape on the hosted project

- Where: `parse_log` takes every exported message that contains `iamb_log ` anywhere and parses
  the rest of it as JSON, with no handling of a failure (`server/restore/restore.py:45-51`); it
  sets `logged_at` only from a number (`:52-55`). In the log-file form it accepts any line that
  ends with `iamb_log {…}`, whatever kind of line it is (`:36`).
- What:
  - Postgres logs a failed cast with the text that failed. On the hosted project the log's
    `event_message` for such a line is the message alone, for example `invalid input syntax for
    type integer: "x"` (seen there for a `pull` with `"limit": "x"`), and a message of several
    lines is one event. So a signed-in account that sends `"base_rev": "iamb_log x"` in a push
    leaves an event that the script takes for a function log line and cannot parse. The script
    stops with a traceback before it has done anything, in the hour the operator needs it.
    Nothing a caller can send makes it accept a forged call: the message always ends with the
    closing quote, and the parser fails on it.
  - The platform's `timestamp` is an ISO string (`2026-11-21T14:44:46.070000`), not a number, so
    every line is ordered by the database's own `at`, and the floor of the new epoch comes from
    it. C18 wants the log system's time for exactly this.
  - The README's step 3 names the source `postgres_logs` and a `POST`; on the endpoint as it is
    today (`GET /v1/projects/<ref>/analytics/endpoints/logs`) the table is `logs`, and
    `postgres_logs` "does not exist".
  - In the log-file form a `STATEMENT:` line that ends with `iamb_log {…}` is accepted as a call
    (shown locally: a forged `delete_account` was parsed). Only somebody who can send SQL text can
    write such a line; no device can, the REST interface sends parameters apart.
- Reproduction (local, from the repository root):

  ```python
  import importlib.util, json, tempfile
  spec = importlib.util.spec_from_file_location("restore", "server/restore/restore.py")
  restore = importlib.util.module_from_spec(spec); spec.loader.exec_module(restore)
  genuine = {"event_message": 'iamb_log {"at": 1795272286071, "event": "remove_member", "user": "a", "group": "b", "person": "c", "answer": "ok"}',
             "timestamp": "2026-11-21T14:44:46.070000"}
  hostile = {"event_message": 'invalid input syntax for type bigint: "iamb_log x"', "timestamp": "2026-11-21T14:45:00.000000"}
  for events in ([genuine], [genuine, hostile]):
      with tempfile.NamedTemporaryFile("w", suffix=".json", delete=False) as f:
          json.dump(events, f)
      try:
          lines = restore.parse_log(f.name)
          print(len(lines), "line(s); logged_at set:", ["logged_at" in l for l in lines])
      except Exception as e:
          print("the script stops:", type(e).__name__, e)
  ```

  Result: `1 line(s); logged_at set: [False]`, then `the script stops: JSONDecodeError Expecting
  value: line 1 column 1 (char 0)`.
- Fix: accept a message only when it begins with the marker (and, in the log-file form, only a
  `LOG:` line); skip and count what does not parse, and print the count in the report; read ISO
  times; correct the README's step 3. See also proposal 8 in §7: commands should not travel
  through the log.

### 16. Scratch tables live in the caller's temporary schema — low, demonstrated locally with a SQL session

The definer functions `create temp table if not exists iamb_feed_pending …` and write to
`pg_temp.…` (`…100200_private_helpers.sql:355-376`). A caller who has made a table of that name
first, with a trigger, has the trigger run as the tables' owner. Shown locally as `authenticated`
in `psql`: Tom made himself an admin of the family from inside the trigger while calling
`set_display_name`. No device can do it: the REST interface runs one function call per request
and no statement of the caller's. It is one step away, though, from any future way to run a
statement as `authenticated`, and `anon` and `authenticated` hold the `TEMPORARY` privilege on the
hosted database. Fix: keep the scratch rows in ordinary tables of `iamb_private` keyed by the
transaction (`pg_current_xact_id()`), deleted with a `WHERE` at the end (which is also the fix for
finding 14); or at least refuse a scratch table the function's owner does not own. The same
reasoning applies to type names with `search_path = ''`: the temporary schema is searched first
for tables and types.

### 17. A socket joined before a revocation keeps receiving pokes — low, demonstrated on the hosted project

The realtime service checks the policy when a channel is joined. A session that had joined its
own topic, and whose device was then revoked, still received the next poke (`{epoch, head}`); a

### 18. No bound in bytes on whole reads and on what a profile may hold — low, demonstrated locally

`rows`, `snapshot` and `fetch_folder` return up to 500 rows a page with no bound in bytes
(`pull` has one: 4.0 MB for 11 entries in the same run). With 200 snippets of 400,000 bytes one
answer of each was 80 MB; 500 rows are 200 MB, and 300 such pages a minute are allowed per
device. The plan counts rows, not bytes: 10,000 snippets of 400 kB are 4 GB of live rows in one
profile, an account can own four profiles, and every edit leaves its replaced version in the history
for 90 days. A member of a family can also put one `folder_revoked` and one `folder_visible` into
every other member's feed with each pair of pushes (29 pairs in a minute from one device with one
shared folder, measured; ten times that with ten shared folders), each of which makes the others'
devices fetch a tree. These are the design's numbers rather than mistakes of the implementation;
they want a byte bound per page and a byte allowance per profile before the server is open to
strangers.

## 6. The advisor's 24 warnings and proposal 10

The advisor on the hosted project shows 24 warnings, all lint 0029 ("Signed-In Users Can Execute
SECURITY DEFINER Function"), one for each definer function of `api`. They are right about what
these functions are: the whole boundary. Each of the 24 runs as the tables' owner, outside every
policy, on arguments a device chose. Whatever they forget is not caught by anything else, and that
is where this review's findings are: `get_conflict` (1), `resolve_conflict` (2, 4), `push` (3, 6,
7, 10, 11), `accept_ownership` (5, 15), `attachment_upload_begin` (6, 8), `accept_invitation`,
`invite`, `create_group`, `hello` (10), `revoke_device`, `sign_out_everywhere` (12). The README
calls the warnings intentional and quotes the advisor's own guidance: "Confirm the function
validates input and limits what it does, then suppress." For the eleven functions just named that
confirmation does not hold today.

My view:

- Keep the pattern. Writes through definer functions that check §4 themselves are a sound design
  for this server; the reading functions being invoker functions under the policies is what kept
  the sweep clean.
- Do not suppress the 24 in one go, and do not treat the count as noise. Suppress a function when
  its findings are fixed and a test holds the fix, with the reason written next to it.
- **Proposal 10** (invoker wrappers in `api`, the definer bodies in a schema that is not exposed)
  would bring the count to zero and change nothing else: the same bodies would run for the same
  callers. The lint would stop seeing them, as it already does not see the seven definer gate
  helpers in `iamb_private`. I advise against it. A count of zero bought that way removes the one
  outside signal that says where the boundary is. If the owner wants a clean advisor, the honest
  route is per-function suppression after review.
- Shrinking the definer surface is worth more than hiding it: `members`, `entitlements` and
  `get_conflict` are readers that became definer functions (correction 1). Each could be an
  invoker function that calls one small definer helper for the one thing the policies cannot give
  (the addresses, the plan, the history rows of a conflict the caller is party to and may read).

### 11.2 Client-visible changes

For the sync client (task 4.4) and the interface. No existing function changed its arguments.

- **`get_conflict`** returns the versions on offer *to the caller*; two parties of one conflict can
  see different lists, and the answer says nothing about the versions left out. It no longer
  answers `forbidden`; a conflict on a row the caller cannot read is `not_found`. A version's
  `display_name` is null when the caller may not read that person's name (§5.13).
- **`resolve_conflict`** answers `not_found` for a party who cannot read the row (also for "keep"),
  `invalid` for a version that is not on offer to the caller, and `forbidden` when the restore is
  a write the caller may not make (it moves a folder to the top level, under another parent or out
  of the caller's access root). A refused restore writes nothing and leaves the conflict open.
- **`conflicts`** and the `conflict` feed entries reach a party only while they can read the row.
- **`push`**: `blocked: invalid` also for an op of the wrong shape (a `base_rev` or `repush_rev`
  that is not a whole number from 0, a `follows` or `cascade` that is not a list, a number no
  column holds; a key with JSON null counts as absent) and for a create whose id is in use by a row
  of another profile; `blocked: too_large` for a cascade list over 20,000 rows; the request-level
  error `invalid` when an op failed in the database (the whole request is undone and counted). A
  delete of a row the caller cannot read is `unchanged` (was `rejected: forbidden`); an op that
  names a profile or group the caller has no grant in is `rejected: parent_gone` (was
  `rejected: forbidden`). `rejected: forbidden` still answers a caller who can see the thing and
  may not change it.
- **The cascade answer** can hold entries in a short form, `{entity, id, rev, deleted: false,
  fetch: true}` without `row`, when the whole rows would pass 4 MiB; the device gets those rows
  with `rows`.
- **`content_by`** is null ("nobody") for a write whose base version the writer could not read.
- **`fetch_folder`, `snapshot`**: a page can hold fewer than 500 rows; follow the cursor until it
  is null, as before.
- **New: `attachment_upload_cancel(device, protocol, upload)`**, by the uploader or whoever manages
  the profile; `not_found` otherwise. `attachment_upload_begin` answers `invalid` for a size of 0
  and `plan_limit` also when the caller has 20 uploads open or their sizes pass the caller's own
  allowance. `api_service.attachment_verified` can answer `plan_limit` (the file does not fit the
  profile; nothing is recorded, and the edge function removes the object).
- **Account calls**: a call that names a group answers a non-member `not_found` before anything
  else is looked at. A repeated `accept_invitation` by somebody who has been removed since answers
  `invitation_invalid`, a repeated `create_group` by a creator who has left answers `invalid`, and
  neither carries `mark`. `accept_ownership` answers `no_offer` once the membership the offer was
  made to has ended; the offer's end is announced to the owner like its withdrawal.
- **`revoke_device`, `sign_out_everywhere`** have a rate of their own (10 a minute per calling
  device). An agent that is answered `rate_limited` all the same should end the sessions at the
  authentication service at once and make the call again later.
- **`session_refused`** also for a session past a time limit set at the authentication service.
- **Scenarios**: `spec/sync/scenarios.toml` has 87 (S85 to S87 are new; S87 has a `resolve` step
  answered `error:forbidden`). No earlier scenario changed.
